Taking CS 374 as an Incoming Sophomore?

[Post] I always assumed the progression would be 225, 233, 341, and then 374. 
A few of my friends said that people they know take 225 in the fall and then 
374 over in spring. I am currently taking 225 over the summer so I could 
technically take 374 in the fall. Is it even beneficial to take 374 in the 
fall (for resume/interview prep)? And would it be better to take with a bunch 
of people I know well?

[Comment] margaretmfleck (CS faculty): What is your math and CS theory 
background like? CS 374 leans heavily on mathematical maturity. If you took 
CS 173 or a similar proof-based discrete math course from a reasonable strong 
CS program and it went smoothly, you will likely do fine. If you struggled with 
proofs or have a weak-ish transfer equivalent of CS 173, then it's a bad idea 
to move quickly forward to CS 374. If your math/theory background is 
questionable, it's probably a good idea to first settle into the coursework 
here and fill in courses like linear algebra. Doing well in CS 225 is a great 
predictor for doing well in CS 233, but not so good at predicting success in 
CS 374. CS 225 is mixed programming/theory, thus doesn't push as hard on the 
math/theory skills.

[Comment] Chickadee3141592: I took 374 my first sophomore semester. It was

[Comment] jeffgerickson (CS 374 instructor): If you have a strong grasp of 
the material in CS 225 and especially CS 173, you should be okay to jump 
right into CS 374. But if the Recursion Fairy isn't your friend, you might 
be better off spending a semester building your discrete math chops. Do not 
under any circumstances take CS 374 and CS 341 in the same semester. Yes, 
CS 374 can help with interview prep. Absolutely take CS 374 with other people 
you know well.

learning Latin — you really aren't going to get anywhere unless YOU understand 
how to get to a solution yourself. It will help for interviews if you are 
interviewing for a position working with algorithms, otherwise it won't.
